# Relocation checklist (working abroad)

Shown when the user has an interview or offer abroad, or asks "checklist dokumen pindah" / "relocation checklist". Tailor it to the
destination, the work pass and whether family comes; check each country-specific item on the official site before stating it, and link
the source. Present as a short checklist with "kapan" (when) for each line. Never ask the user to send copies of documents to Claude.

## Before accepting
- Written offer and contract in a language you understand; who pays the visa, flights and relocation.
- Work pass route and its salary minimum (fit-check filters.md); for family, the dependant rule and documents it needs.
- Take-home vs living cost (career-autopilot-offer-check, step 3).

## Documents (start early; some take weeks)
- Passport valid at least 6–12 months beyond the planned stay (check the destination's rule), with blank pages; one for each family member.
- Degree and transcript: legalisation or apostille. Indonesia is a party to the Apostille Convention: apostille through the Ministry of
  Law (AHU online) for countries that accept it; others may need embassy legalisation. Some jobs need a qualification assessment
  (e.g. Australia skills assessment, Germany recognition) or professional registration.
- Police certificate: for Indonesians, SKCK from POLRI (the version for going abroad); other citizenships: their national equivalent.
- Family: marriage certificate and children's birth certificates, with sworn translation and legalisation/apostille as the destination asks.
- Medical check or vaccination records if the pass requires them (only at clinics the authority names, never one a recruiter picks for a fee).
- Employment references or service letters, payslips if the visa or the employer asks.
- CV and certificates in the destination's format (career-autopilot-cv-builder, Country formats).

## For Indonesian citizens (Pekerja Migran Indonesia)
- Register the job in SISKOP2MI (siskop2mi.bp2mi.go.id) before leaving, through a P3MI agency listed as active or the jalur mandiri
  route; official placement charges no recruitment fee.
- BPJS Ketenagakerjaan for migrant workers (jaminan sosial PMI), and the pre-departure orientation (OPP) when required.
- Report to the Indonesian embassy or consulate after arrival (Lapor Diri, e.g. through the Peduli WNI service).
- Tax: Indonesians living abroad more than 183 days are usually no longer Indonesian tax residents; confirm with DJP before leaving.

## Money and first month
- Budget for the first 1–2 months before the first salary: deposit (often 1–2 months' rent), agent fee, school deposits, transport, food.
- A way to receive salary and send money home (bank account in the new country; compare remittance fees).
- Health insurance from day one for everyone in the family.

## After arrival
- Collect the work pass card or residence permit; register address where required.
- School places for children (apply early; international schools have waiting lists).
- Keep copies of every document and the contract in your own Drive.
