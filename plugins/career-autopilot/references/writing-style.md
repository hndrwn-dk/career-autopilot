# Writing style for everything the user sends

Applies to application emails, replies to recruiters, cover letters, free-text form answers, LinkedIn notes
and follow-ups. The goal: it reads like the user wrote it on a good day, in their own words.

## 1. Voice source, in order

1. Profile `writing_sample` (a few emails or messages the user wrote): match its sentence length, greeting and sign-off, formality, and punctuation. The sample overrides the rules below where they conflict.
2. Profile `tone` (e.g. "formal", "friendly but short", "Bahasa Indonesia baku").
3. Default: plain, polite, specific. In the language of the posting or the message being answered.

Offer once, after the first draft the user edits: "Mau saya simpan gaya tulisan Anda dari email ini?" Save only what the user approves as `writing_sample`.

## 2. Content rules

- Every claim comes from Profile or Stories. No invented numbers, tools, titles or results.
- One concrete example beats three adjectives. Prefer "migrated 40 TB without downtime" to "proven track record".
- Answer what the posting or the recruiter asked, in that order. Put the main point in the first two sentences.
- Short: an application email under 180 words, a reply under 120, a cover letter 150–250.

## 3. Patterns to remove before showing the draft

- "Not X but Y" / "bukan hanya X, tetapi juga Y" contrasts that add weight without adding a fact.
- One-line closers that repeat the paragraph ("That's what I bring.", "Itulah yang saya tawarkan.").
- Lists of three adjectives or three examples when one would do.
- Em dashes and en dashes; use a comma, period or colon. (Keep them only if `writing_sample` uses them.)
- Inflated words: passionate, dynamic, results-driven, synergy, leverage, spearheaded, pivotal, robust, seamless, delve, testament, landscape; Indonesian equivalents such as "sangat bersemangat", "berdedikasi tinggi", "dinamis" used as filler.
- Openers that stage the point: "I am writing to express my keen interest", "Dengan hormat, melalui surat ini saya bermaksud…" (unless the target country or company expects that form; see 4).
- Chatbot residue: "I hope this helps", "Feel free to reach out", "Semoga bermanfaat".
- Bold text, emojis and bullet lists in emails unless the recruiter's own message uses them.
- Repeated sentence openings ("I… I… I…", "Saya… Saya…").

## 4. Local conventions (adjust, do not over-apply)

- Indonesia, formal application: a short "Dengan hormat," opening and "Hormat saya," closing are normal; keep the body plain.
- Japan, Germany and the Gulf: more formal salutations; keep the body concrete.
- US, UK, Australia, Singapore: first-name replies are fine once the recruiter uses your first name.
- Follow the posting's instructions exactly (subject line, reference number, documents).

## 5. Self-check (silent, before showing)

Read the draft as the recipient. Would a busy recruiter know in 10 seconds who this is, which role, and why they fit?
Does any sentence sound like a template? Rewrite it with a specific fact or cut it.
